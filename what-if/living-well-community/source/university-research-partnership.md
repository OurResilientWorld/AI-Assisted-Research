# University research partnership and future commercialisation

## Selecting a suitable university partner

Living Well would seek a university offering suitable courses and research capability in exercise, nutrition, healthy ageing and evaluation. **No university is nominated at this stage.** The appropriate partner will depend on the pilot location, community needs, available staff and facilities, research interests, costs and agreement on responsibilities and rights.

After local feasibility work, Living Well would prepare a common partnership brief and assess interested universities against:

- relevant teaching and research expertise, with a named research lead and access to trial design, health economics and longitudinal research skills;
- practical local reach, accessible participant involvement and capacity to support initial research and longer follow-up;
- scientific independence, ethics and data safeguards, and willingness to report all findings;
- full costs, available resources, timing and a sustainable funding arrangement;
- acceptable intellectual-property, community-use, publication and future commercialisation terms.

The management committee would assess proposals within the member-approved direction and budget framework before authorising an agreement. If no suitable arrangement is available, revise the research plan or timing before making commitments. No university or academic has been contacted for this proposal.

University partnership covers research from design to long-term outcomes. Clinical assessment, professional review, safety decisions and referrals remain with qualified service providers. Students may contribute through separately approved, supervised placements or research projects. Consent, competence, supervision and educational obligations must be agreed; student participation is neither free core staffing nor a reason to add universal dietitian appointments.

## Research program and funding gates

Living Well's members help choose meaningful questions and interpret practical implications. The university controls scientific methods and analysis within the agreed protocol, with independent methods review where its team also designed the intervention. A research steering group includes participant representatives, Living Well, university investigators and an independent methods/ethics adviser. Donors, suppliers and prospective licensees cannot suppress findings or determine clinical care.

| Phase | Work and outputs | Decision and funding boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Initial research before enrolment | Co-design; evidence review; local need/access study; comparator and outcome choices; feasibility, data plan, ethics review and analysis plan | Cost and approve the work package before it starts; use the $25,000 discovery and $120,000 initial/pilot research allowances without double charging |
| Pilot: baseline, 6 and 12 months | Recruitment/consent, function, personal goals, wellbeing, falls/harms, access, burden and full resource use; publish feasibility and uncertainty | Pilot research must be funded and approved before enrolment; a 100-person pilot is not proof of rare-event safety or long-term effectiveness |
| Comparative study and 24-month follow-up | Use pilot data to design/power a fair comparison and estimate added benefit and cost-effectiveness | Separate approved protocol and secured research budget; not claimed to fit automatically inside the pilot allowance |
| Years 3–5 | Durable function, participation, family effects, role-specific care capacity and actual renewal; follow consenting people after service exit or age 79 | Separate funded schedule and recontact/withdrawal arrangements; track attrition and selection bias |
| Years 6–10, conditional | Persistence, equity, later care and performance across locations | Renew funding, ethics and data agreements before extending; 20-year model scenarios remain projections, not promised funded observations |

The existing comparative plan tests full Living Well against current services and equivalent professional support without watch/AI. All Living Well service participants still receive the agreed watch/AI; comparator arms are distinct research offers. Preregister outcomes and analysis, agree meaningful thresholds with participants/clinicians, report null or adverse findings and missing data, and use independent outcome assessment where feasible. Research consent is optional and separate from receiving care; declining or withdrawing cannot reduce the service.

Obtain the appropriate university ethics review and approvals before human research starts, using the then-applicable [NHMRC National Statement](https://www.nhmrc.gov.au/research-policy/ethics/national-statement-ethical-conduct-human-research). Plan consent and accessibility for participants with differing capacity, language and digital needs. A research problem must have a named route to the clinical lead where safety is affected, without giving researchers unrestricted clinical-record access.

## Heads of agreement before research starts

This is a negotiation specification, not an executed contract or a statement of university policy. Living Well and the university must agree terms acceptable to both institutions and obtain legal, privacy and ethics advice.

| Topic | Proposed terms to settle |
|---|---|
| Parties and work | Living Well Inc. and the university, named accountable leads, phased deliverables, sites, milestones, permitted subcontractors and supervision |
| Full cost and resources | Staff time, overhead, participant expenses, travel, accessible participation, data systems, ethics, analysis, publication and retention; cash and any confirmed in-kind contributions separately valued; no double billing with clinical contracts |
| Independence | Registered methods, access for agreed analysis, independent assessment, publication of favourable, null and harmful findings; funder and supplier conflicts disclosed; scientific conclusions cannot be changed by a member vote |
| Publication | Plain-language reports to participants and members; open methods/results where lawful; proposed 30-day confidentiality/IP review and a single justified patent-filing extension up to 60 additional days; no indefinite veto, including over theses; negotiate before signing |
| Data stewardship | Identify each custodian and permitted access; separate care/research records, minimisation, security, retention, breach response, linkage approvals, withdrawal limits and end-of-project custody. Use participant information only within valid consent, ethics and law. No sale of personal data or supplier AI training by default |
| Existing intellectual property | List each party's background IP, third-party software/data licences and restrictions; ownership remains with its owner. Grant only the access needed for the agreed research |
| New intellectual property | Agree ownership by type and contribution before work starts, including inventions, software, protocols and databases. Do not assume all university work belongs to Living Well, or all collaborative outputs to the university. Resolve employee, student and third-party rights explicitly; avoid undefined joint ownership |
| Community use | Seek a continuing, non-exclusive, royalty-free licence for Living Well's agreed not-for-profit service, evaluation and accessible training use of project outputs, with required background rights. Scope, maintenance and sublicensing to delivery partners must be negotiated. Existing third-party restrictions must be disclosed |
| Exit and disputes | Staged negotiation/mediation, suspension for safety or breach, care continuity, completion/publication of existing analyses, data custody and survival of agreed use rights after termination; fund these duties |

Once a prospective partner is identified, check its current research, intellectual-property and commercialisation policies and negotiate the agreement through its authorised office. Published university agreement examples can inform the discussion but do not establish the selected partner's terms or any rights for Living Well.

## Separate agreement before any commercialisation

Agree the process at research inception; approve a transaction only once the actual opportunity, rights and risks are known. The agreement must apply to commercial use by the university, Living Well or a third party of **joint project outputs and Living Well background contributions**. It does not give Living Well control of unrelated university research.

1. Identify the invention/software/service, owners, third-party rights and contributors. Obtain a written public-benefit and financial appraisal, with independent advice where an investigator, committee member or supplier could benefit.
2. Members deliberate and decide Living Well's material commercialisation proposal; the committee verifies legality and solvency; authorised officers of both institutions execute the licence. Student/inventor rights and any other owners' permissions must be resolved.
3. Prefer non-exclusive licensing where practical. Any exclusivity needs a justified field, territory and term, access commitments, development milestones and termination/reversion for non-performance. Protect Living Well's continuing community-use licence and research/teaching rights.
4. Define patent, development and licensing costs; which deductions are allowed; contributions; revenue-sharing formula; payment timing; audit rights and dispute handling. Negotiate a fair share rather than inventing a percentage now. Living Well reinvests its net proceeds in its not-for-profit purposes; private distributions to its members are excluded.
5. Set affordable-access commitments and public-interest safeguards. Retain publication rights and time-limited IP review. Commercial use of personal or linked data is not granted merely by licensing an algorithm: separately check consent, ethics, privacy and necessary approvals or renewed consent.
6. Review association eligibility, tax, charity/fundraising restrictions and conflicts before execution. Any new entity or change of structure requires a separate member decision and legal advice; no automatic spin-out is proposed.

## Financial reconciliation and outstanding decisions

Initial/pilot research retains the **$120,000 allowance before the 10% reserve**. This is not a university quote and does not fund an assumed decade of research. Discovery remains $25,000 and formation/setup $90,000, including the $15,000 pre-appeal formation allocation. Allocate university feasibility work between these existing lines once, using a signed scope and cost schedule.

Keep the gross pilot crowdfunding baseline at $891,508 in 2026 dollars (about $932,000 at assumed 2027–28 prices) only as a comparison reference. If a full-cost university proposal exceeds the relevant allowance, raise the verified campaign target or explicitly redesign the research scope before recruitment; do not divert necessary clinical support. Required outcome follow-up commitments must have finance secured before they are promised. A signed framework may describe later phases conditionally, but it cannot make unfunded obligations disappear.

No royalties, government research grants, unconfirmed university subsidy or free students are included in the non-government funding model. Future commercialisation is a contingent possibility, not a reason to claim financial viability. Record any university contribution and its ultimate funding source before accepting it within the agreed non-government boundary. Costs and rights remain unresolved until the parties agree them.
