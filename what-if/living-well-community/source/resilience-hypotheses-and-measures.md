# Five resilience hypotheses and decision rules

Artificial intelligence (AI) coaching is included in the service and assessed against equivalent human support.

The evaluation tests five linked mechanisms for the Living Well service. The thresholds below are proposed Product Owner/clinical/evaluation decisions, not measured effects or universally accepted minimums. Establish and register them before the relevant study. A pilot of 100 tests feasibility; its size does not establish efficacy, rare-event safety or subgroup effects. Independent statisticians must set the comparative trial size using variability, clustering, attrition and plausible effect bounds. Do not search outcomes after seeing results.

The common baseline is a pre-enrolment functional assessment, four-week time/use diary, current services/costs and consented carer assessment. Assess at 6, 12 and 24 months after enrolment; collect safety and resource use monthly; repeat annually where funded. For a July 2027 start, these are January/July 2028 and July 2029, with longer capacity/family work extending to 2030–32 and beyond. Dates move if recruitment is delayed. Report rurality, income, sex, disability, age band and digital confidence; small subgroups need intervals and qualitative evidence, not unsupported subgroup success claims.

Twenty-year scenario cannot verify this |

For every threshold report the effect estimate and uncertainty interval, raw denominators, missingness, adverse outcomes and alternative explanations. A point estimate above a target is not a pass when uncertainty includes material harm/no benefit. No arbitrary aggregate resilience score combines failed and successful hypotheses. Different outcomes can justify different decisions: useful function with no net capacity benefit may support a revised health proposition but not the national capacity claim.

## Attribution and measurement safeguards

A1 is the full package versus existing services; A2 is the package versus equivalent professional support without technology; A3 compares resource-equivalent alternatives. Randomised allocation is preferred where feasible and ethical; otherwise state residual confounding and do not label associations causal. For unpaid activity, distinguish participant enjoyment from useful output to others, count simultaneous tasks once and subtract displaced activity. Record caregiver support time even when the caregiver likes helping. Respect a participant's choice to use better health for leisure.

For capacity, retain role-specific ledgers and the added care caused by detecting needs. Report actual paid budget effects separately. Do not count the same hour as hospital savings and additional independent-living output without an explicit boundary. University research teams should have access to incident and dropout records and freedom to report null findings. No payer may suppress adverse results.

No baseline participant measurements or whole-programme effects have yet been established. Community governance is additionally assessed through the decision-rights and outcome matrix; a vote or campaign success does not prove these five hypotheses.

The university research partnership is responsible for the agreed evaluation work across these measures; qualified service providers retain individual clinical responsibility. Research phases, independence and long-term funding follow the [partnership proposal](university-research-partnership.md).
