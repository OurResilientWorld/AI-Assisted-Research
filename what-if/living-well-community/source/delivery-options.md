# Living Well governance and delivery choices

## A community-governed New South Wales (NSW) association

Recommend a new **Living Well Inc.**, incorporated under the **Associations Incorporation Act 2009 (NSW)** with a custom not-for-profit constitution. The association owns the program, contracts with every partner, employs or contracts management and retains surplus for its purposes. Its proposed objects are older people's mobility, independence, connection and public-benefit learning. On winding up, remaining assets would go to an eligible similar-purpose not-for-profit, subject to applicable law and donor restrictions. No distribution to members is proposed.

This choice puts a local community purpose within a NSW legal structure. The participatory constitution, transparent practice and members' enforceable rights carry the philosophy; the legal label alone does not guarantee it. [NSW guidance](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/incorporated-associations/about-incorporated-associations).

## Proposed constitutional design

These are design recommendations for member consultation and legal drafting, not provisions already adopted or claims that every detail is required by law.

| Decision right | Proposed Living Well rule |
|---|---|
| Join and leave | Adult participants, carers and local supporters may apply under clear purpose-based criteria; accessible fee waivers; written reasons and appeal for refusal. Service and research participation remain separate. Illness or inability to volunteer never removes membership automatically. |
| Equal voice | One natural-person member, one vote. Donor organisations and commercial partners cannot buy votes or reserved seats. Supporters can help a person participate without taking over their decision. |
| Set the agenda | Any member may submit a proposal with an accessible explanation. A published register shows response and reasons. Five members or 5% of membership, whichever is fewer, may request a special meeting under the proposed constitution. |
| Deliberate and decide | Give members plain-language options, costs, uncertainties and conflicts before a vote. Members approve strategic priorities, the annual budget framework and material expansion. Routine procurement remains delegated. |
| Protect the purpose | Constitutional changes follow statutory special-resolution rules. Material exclusive IP licences, transfer of core program assets or changes to access principles require a member decision before commitment, subject to lawful committee duties. |
| Elect and remove | Propose a seven-person elected committee, two-year staggered terms and maximum three consecutive terms; aim for at least three people with participant/carer lived experience. Lawful removal and fair replacement procedures are written into the constitution. Skills gaps may be filled by paid advisers without buying decision rights. |
| Review and challenge | Members inspect non-confidential reasons, financial reports, conflicts and performance; request independent review; use fair complaints and appeal processes. Report how review changes a decision. |
| Act urgently | Clinicians stop unsafe care immediately. Management acts within emergency delegations. The committee reviews urgent action within 30 days and reports appropriately to members. No vote can require unlawful, insolvent or unsafe conduct. |

A proposed general-meeting quorum is 20% of eligible members, rounded up, with a minimum of five; consult members and legally settle notice, remote voting, adjournment and election rules before incorporation. Ordinary decisions use a simple majority of valid votes. Statutory special resolutions require at least three-quarters of votes cast and proper notice/procedure; constitution changes also require the applicable lodgement/registration process. [NSW resolutions guidance](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/incorporated-associations/running-an-incorporated-association/resolutions).

The committee remains responsible for managing the association lawfully and maintaining financial viability. If a member decision cannot be implemented lawfully or safely, record the reason, obtain independent advice and return a compliant alternative for decision; it is not an unrestricted committee veto over member policy. [Committee responsibilities](https://www.nsw.gov.au/business-and-economy/incorporated-associations/running-an-incorporated-association/management-committee-obligations).

## One World principles in ordinary operations

The One World Mandate and story emphasise voluntary cooperation, transparency, members setting priorities and testing proposals with open models. The contemporary concept brief adds structured deliberation and AI assisting reasoning. The supplied resilience taxonomy separates agenda, decision, veto and review powers. These inform the rules above; the exact thresholds and terms are Living Well design proposals. The story is a vision, not measured evidence. Historical AI decision-making and token mechanisms are not adopted.

Publish accessible proposal briefs, aggregate evidence, model assumptions, decision reasons, remuneration principles and declared conflicts. Keep personal records, confidential complaints and legitimate IP protection controlled. Offer telephone, paper and supported meetings; budget accessibility and participation work. Measure who actually contributes and whether one group dominates. Community control is a testable governance claim, not an assumption satisfied by an annual election.

## Form and manage the association

Secure seed underwriting before paid work. Founders document who bears pre-incorporation liabilities. Prepare the purpose, custom constitution, proposed name and management arrangements with prospective members. NSW registration requires at least five members; an adult public officer resident in NSW; and a committee of at least three adults, with at least three residing in Australia. These are minimum legal arrangements; the proposed committee above has seven members. [Service NSW registration](https://www.service.nsw.gov.au/transaction/apply-to-register-an-incorporated-association).

After registration, members approve the initial direction and budget framework. The committee appoints paid management, a qualified clinical lead and a finance function, sets spending and dual-authorisation controls, obtains insurance and confirms fundraising compliance. It approves safe, solvent execution of the community-approved campaign. Living Well negotiates and manages participant, provider, community, technology, funder and university contracts. Clinical decisions remain with qualified professionals; research findings remain with researchers. Separate complaints and independent assurance are retained.

## Three delivery arrangements within the association

All arrangements retain the same participant service and university partnership. These are delivery choices, not alternative owners.

| Arrangement | Benefit | Main cost/risk | Pilot judgement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contracted local provider network | Draws on existing qualified capacity and venues | Coordination, travel, continuity, contract oversight | Preferred starting option, subject to full-cost quotes and capacity |
| Employed multidisciplinary team | Direct scheduling and supervision | Salaries, leave, recruitment, underused capacity | Compare if local contracted capacity is inadequate |
| Employed coordination/clinical core with contracted local teams | Common standards with local reach | Duplicate management and unclear handoffs | Test for later multi-site delivery; allocate each duty once |

Compare these with current services and spending an equivalent private budget on improving access to existing support. Those comparators are not reduced Living Well offers. Obtain at least two complete proposals against one service specification.

## Cost and suitability gates

D1 now denotes the NSW association base overhead allowance: $120,000/$180,000/$410,000 annually at 100/250/1,000 places, before reserve. D2 retains $100,000/$150,000/$350,000 solely as a lower-overhead sensitivity for the same association. Neither prices the three delivery arrangements or proves one is cheaper. Formation/setup remains $90,000 before reserve, including $15,000 advanced through seed. Participation support, lawful governance and university contracting must be explicitly allocated in quotations.

Before filing, obtain advice on eligibility for the proposed service and fees: NSW guidance positions associations for small-scale not-for-profit activities. Before interstate expansion or commercial licensing, review structure suitability, registration, fundraising, tax and reporting obligations. If unsuitable, return options to members; no automatic conversion or dilution of community control is authorised. Charity registration and deductible-gift endorsement are separate. No name availability, incorporation or legal clearance is claimed.
