# Funding choices after startup

## Two different funding decisions

Startup crowdfunding is required for D1 and D2 and funds the defined pilot before enrolment. The next decision is how the service pays its bills after that period. A delivery organisation can use more than one lawful private payer, while member control and clinical independence remain intact.

All recurring terms below are **hypotheses in 2026 dollars**, beginning after the fully donation-funded first year. The 20% concession/equity share is a test allocation, not a measure of local need. Participants must receive clear notice of later pricing and a supported exit/transition if they do not renew.

| Recurring funding test | Proposed payers and reason to pay | Annual mechanism | Decision authority / main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Members plus sponsors — central illustration | Participants value coordinated support; private sponsors support defined aggregate community outcomes |80% pay $200/month, 20% pay $60; sponsor pays $1,000 per place/year | The committee sets affordable access with member input; sponsor signs contract with no individual data/control. Renewal and affordability untested |
| Members only | Participants purchase the service directly, with cross-subsidy |80% pay $300/month, 20% pay $60 | Requires sufficient affordable demand; fees cannot be justified by model arithmetic alone |
| Insurer plus equity donors | Authorised insurer may purchase an eligible service; private donors fund uninsured access | $3,600 per place/year: insurer 80%, private equity-access donors 20%; no participant fee | Full-bundle eligibility and purchasing power require written advice/contracts; buyer concentration and equitable selection matter |
| Members plus recurring supporters | Members contribute and many donors support access/continuity | $200/$60 member split plus $1,000 gross gifts/place/year, assumed 92% net | Repeat gifts, collection cost and acquisition effort untested; no extra votes or participant storytelling conditions |

Sponsors, insurers and donors are purchasers/supporters, not automatically owners. Investment or debt finances a cash need and may require repayment; neither is recurring operating income. Public grants, hidden public programme labour, routine billing of core programme support to Medicare and personal-data sales are excluded from the financing plan. Existing ordinary medical entitlements remain available and any attributable public fiscal effects are recorded separately.

The full CSV repeats these tests for every delivery option, three scales and three cost assumptions. Ownership is held separate from revenue: changing who pays does not prove that service cost, access or outcomes improve. An organisation’s actual legal status may restrict a particular funding route; advice and contract terms must resolve this before reliance.

## Evidence needed to select recurring funding

Test actual full-cost renewal, not favourable survey responses. For insurers, establish lawful eligible populations and complete-bundle purchasing authority. For sponsors, define deliverables without selling health data. For donors, test net acquisition/retention and restricted-fund suitability. For households, examine affordability and time burden alongside satisfaction. Count funder concentration, cancellation rights and settlement timing.

Collect enough evidence before pilot renewal to avoid a sudden funding gap. If recurring funding fails, do not assume another emergency appeal will rescue commitments. Use the funded transition/exit arrangements and return the service proposition to the committee and members. The [viability evaluation](viability-evaluation.md) assesses these uncertainties with the delivery evidence.

The insurer/equity-access illustration assumes direct contracted equity-access gifts with no platform or collection deduction; the full $180,000 at 250 places is usable cash. Supporter gifts instead pass through the assumed 8% deduction, yielding $230,000 net from $250,000 gross. These are different proposed receipt channels, not verified charges. Apply actual collection costs to either route before contracting.

D1 means the New South Wales (NSW) association base overhead; D2 is a lower-overhead sensitivity for the same association, not another legal form or a quoted delivery alternative. University initial/pilot work must be quoted before the campaign; longer research phases need separate secured funding. No royalty or unconfirmed university contribution balances this model.
