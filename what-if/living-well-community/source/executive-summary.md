# Living Well — proposal at a glance

Living Well would help volunteers aged 65–79 remain mobile, independent and connected by coordinating personal exercise/nutrition support, periodic professional review, a smartwatch and individual artificial intelligence (AI) coaching.

The proposed organisation is **Living Well Inc., a new New South Wales (NSW) not-for-profit incorporated association**. Members set strategic priorities and the budget framework, elect and scrutinise the management committee, and decide material expansion and commercialisation proposals. The committee remains legally accountable; paid management negotiates every partner arrangement.

A university partnership would cover initial research, pilot evaluation and separately funded long-term outcomes. A suitable university would be selected after feasibility work, considering relevant courses, research expertise, local reach, capacity, costs and acceptable partnership terms. No institution is nominated. Proposed terms protect research independence, participant data, continued community use of project outputs and fair benefit-sharing if research is later commercialised.

Startup crowdfunding is mandatory. The illustrative 100-person first year needs about $892,000 gross in 2026 dollars, or $932,000 at assumed 2027–28 prices, including formation/preparation, evaluation, reserves and a cash buffer. Private seed is required before paid work and included once in that target. No funds or partners are committed.

The most grounded expected benefit is improved or maintained everyday function for some participants; connection and confidence are plausible further benefits. Net family time, public savings and financial sustainability need separate evidence. Expansion follows comparative outcomes and funded capacity.

Read the concise four-part [business case](business-case.md): Understand the Business Case; Implementing the Business Case; Managing the Program; The Outcomes. Detailed calculations and resilience tests remain in the supporting analysis. Viability is evaluated at the end, without changing the participant service to make the budget balance.
