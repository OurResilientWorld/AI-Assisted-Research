# Essential startup crowdfunding

## Purpose and position in startup

**All delivery arrangements must complete a successful startup crowdfunding stage before the pilot begins.** The campaign gives the community a concrete proposition to support: a defined participant service, an accountable organisation, a funded period, equitable access and independent evaluation. Raising money is a funding milestone; participant outcomes and resilience are assessed separately.

The proposed base is a fully donation-funded 100-person, 12-month pilot. No compulsory participant fee, insurer payment or corporate service-purchase receipt is counted again for that pilot year. Before joining, people are told that later participation depends on a separately funded renewal model; continuing fees, subsidies and handover arrangements must be agreed transparently. No new clinical services are added by this funding choice.

## Campaign sequence and responsibilities

| Step | Responsible party | Required result |
|---|---|---|
| Prepare the proposition | Community convening group with clinical/finance input | Agreed problem, outcomes, service, delivery shortlist and preliminary budget |
| Secure seed underwriting | Accountable entity and private underwriter | Paid feasibility/campaign preparation, documented loss allocation and any reimbursement terms |
| Establish fundraising authority | Committee, independent advisers and platform | Lawful appeal, truthful claims, privacy, restricted-fund rules, payout and refund terms |
| Community deliberation and campaign decision | Members and accountable Living Well management committee | Members approve scope, priorities and the budget framework; the committee confirms lawful and solvent fundraising |
| Run campaign | Paid fundraising lead reporting to committee and members | Net cleared cash at or above the verified minimum; donor concentration and restrictions reported |
| Release pilot funding | Committee and independent finance/clinical assurance | Funds usable for every obligation, contracts safe, evaluation/exit provision funded |
| Report and renew | Finance lead, university research team and members | Use of money, participation/outcomes and next-period funding decision published to members/donors |

The committee cannot substitute expressions of interest or platform pledges for cleared funds. Delayed payout delays service commitments. If the campaign misses its threshold, do not start; follow agreed refund or donor-approved alternative-use terms. A later smaller proposal requires fresh service costing, community decision and donor terms, not an improvised reduction of promised care.

## Budget before the appeal

Illustrative pre-appeal needs are $25,000 feasibility, $20,000 campaign preparation/management and $15,000 for early formation drawn from the $90,000 setup allowance. Together they require $66,000 seed including a 10% reserve, in 2026 dollars. The $15,000 allocation is an unquoted planning assumption, not an additional cost. The seed underwriter must finance this before paid feasibility and formation. Founders document pre-incorporation liabilities and spending limits; after registration the Living Well management committee accepts only approved obligations before any reimbursement. Living Well itself runs the appeal and negotiates the delivery contracts. In the base, the campaign reimburses that seed after success; if it fails, the underwriter bears the agreed loss. No underwriter is committed. If seed is instead an irrevocable gift, revise the appeal target and records so it is counted once. People are not asked to work unpaid to make the campaign viable.

Total setup remains $90,000, of which $15,000 is financed before the appeal; initial/pilot university research is provisionally $120,000. After seed reimbursement of $66,000, remaining setup of $75,000 and evaluation of $120,000 require $214,500 including reserve. Total preparation/setup/evaluation stays $280,500, with no duplicated formation charge. Setup covers entity/contracts, process design, training and technology integration; participant assessment/onboarding is separately included in service costs. Any overlapping quotation must be corrected before final budget approval. Campaign costs are paid expenses, not participant services.

## Minimum appeal target

The base uses a 5% platform/payment allowance and 3% collection-loss/refund allowance, both of gross donations. Thus 92% is usable before paid campaign work already included above. These are test assumptions, not a selected platform tariff. Verify settlement, taxation, chargeback and restricted-gift conditions before setting a live target.

**Net funding needed = first-year service cost + $280,500 preparation/setup/evaluation + a three-month first-year service-cost buffer. Gross appeal target = net funding needed ÷0.92.** The buffer is held cash, not another expense. Because the base appeal fully funds the pilot, year-one member/sponsor/insurer receipts are zero in the cash model. Actual later receipts cannot be promised or counted twice against that already funded period.

Negative outcomes and funding-readiness conclusions are assessed in the final viability section.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | $431,750 | $280,500 | $107,938 | $820,188 | $891,508 | $931,925 |

For the D1 association base, the gross target is about $891,508 in 2026 dollars or $931,925 in 2027–28 dollars. At a hypothetical $100 gift, the constant-price target needs 8,916 transactions; neither this average nor donor availability is evidenced. Founding donations, signed matching gifts and smaller gifts can contribute, but matches count only when payable and only once. Wealth or gift size buys no extra member votes or clinical priority.

The model includes 100/250/1,000-place targets at lower/midpoint/upper service allowances. Those larger scales show funding exposure, not an invitation to begin with 1,000 people. Any future smaller pilot must preserve safe service and independent evaluation; it cannot reduce arbitrary costs merely to produce an appealing fundraising target.

## Failure and settlement tests

The following fixes the **D1 midpoint 100-place target in 2026 dollars** and changes what is collected or retained. Shortfall means missing usable cash against the same promised programme. The 15% deduction case requires a higher gross target; collecting 100% of the old target is then insufficient.

| Scenario | Net collected | Shortfall | Funding gate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100% collected, 8% deductions | $820,188 | $0 | Funding amount only passes |
| 50% collected, 8% deductions | $410,094 | $410,094 | Delay / do not start |
| No gifts | $0 | $820,188 | Delay / do not start |
| 100% collected, 15% deductions | $757,782 | $62,406 | Delay / do not start |

A three-month settlement delay means a three-month delay to the launch unless separately authorised bridge finance and its cost are fully secured. The base finances no post-start payout delay because the release gate requires usable cash first. Registration, clinical readiness and evaluation checks remain required even after the amount is raised.

## Fairness, oversight and continuity

The campaign funds the programme as a whole. Access must not depend on a participant telling a public personal story, attracting their own donors or promising unpaid work. Give supporters clear information on beneficiary selection, costs, evaluation, restrictions and unused funds. Respect withdrawal from fundraising communications. Health records are kept separate from donor records and are not sold.

An accountable committee retains responsibility even when a platform administers donations. Check platform charges, payout and donor-data arrangements, and ensure funds reach the intended organisation. Charity registration alone does not establish deductible-gift status. [ACNC crowdfunding guidance](https://www.acnc.gov.au/tools/guides/crowdfunding-and-charities). Applicable state/territory fundraising rules, tax treatment and truthful claims require advice before an appeal; this proposal does not itself authorise fundraising.

Recurring supporter donations after the pilot are an optional continuing funding route, separately tested against member contributions, sponsors and insurer purchases. **They do not replace or make optional the essential startup campaign.** Donor renewal and annual paid campaign/supporter administration must cover their own costs. Restricted startup gifts cannot silently become unrestricted long-term operating funds.

D1 means the New South Wales (NSW) association base overhead; D2 is a lower-overhead sensitivity for the same association, not another legal form or a quoted delivery alternative. University initial/pilot work must be quoted before the campaign; longer research phases need separate secured funding. No royalty or unconfirmed university contribution balances this model.
