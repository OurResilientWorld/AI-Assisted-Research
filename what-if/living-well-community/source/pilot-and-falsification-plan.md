# Startup, pilot and evidence-led expansion

## University partnership throughout

The university partner co-designs initial research and leads the independent evaluation and long-term outcomes program under the [partnership agreement](university-research-partnership.md). Clinical providers retain care responsibilities. No university is committed. Separate funding is needed for comparative and longitudinal phases; the $120,000 allowance is for initial/pilot research, subject to quotation.

## Stage 1 — define the local proposition

Before paid feasibility begins, secure private seed underwriting with documented founder liability before incorporation and Living Well management committee oversight after registration, with agreed spending limits and loss/reimbursement terms. Then convene prospective participants/carers, community members and qualified providers to specify the problem, accessible service and desired outcomes. Map current services and include people who decline fees or technology. A proposed 8–12 week feasibility phase uses around 30 participant/carer interviews, provider cost proposals and authorised prospective payer discussions. This is a qualitative design input, not a national demand survey.

Compare current services and improving their accessibility with the delivery arrangements within Living Well. Form Living Well and appoint its committee and management before it signs delivery contracts. Agree member rights, full service specification, cost boundaries, clinical/privacy responsibilities, evaluation design and exit conditions. Test household affordability and recurring prices openly, including what changes after a donation-funded first year. No promise of health improvement or unpaid contribution is used to recruit interest.

## Stage 2 — required crowdfunding

Using the seed underwriting secured before Stage 1, approve a truthful, costed campaign under the accountable entity. Crowdfunding is required under all delivery arrangements. Set the minimum cleared net target to cover the defined first-year service, preparation/setup/evaluation and buffer. The base pilot has no additional participant fees or ordinary sponsor/insurer receipts. Campaign terms must explain future renewal, restrictions, loss of target, delay, refunds, reporting and unused funds.

The proposed campaign/preparation period is 8–12 weeks after feasibility, but clearance and readiness determine the start date. Failure or settlement delay postpones service; it does not permit spending pledged money or omitting safe care. Members approve priorities and the budget framework; the Living Well management committee confirms lawful, safe and solvent execution. These are separate from this draft’s review.

## Stage 3 — readiness and a bounded 100-person pilot

A first pilot could cover one metropolitan and one nearby regional catchment, with 12 months of delivery and separately planned follow-up. Report ages 65–69, 70–74, 75–79, income, disability, language/digital confidence and rural access. A regional pilot does not establish remote feasibility. Clinical suitability and a supported referral pathway govern inclusion; there is no low-risk selection merely to improve results.

Before enrolment, verify cleared finance, lawful fundraising/use of funds, contracted paid capacity, insurance, safe escalation, a tested technology/data chain, accessible setup and an exit/handover plan. The service comprises personal assessment, suitable exercise/nutrition support, professional review, a watch and individual AI; research measurement does not create extra standard clinical appointments. Research participation is optional and separately consented.

Proposed outcome measurements are baseline, 6 and 12 months, with monthly safety/resource recording and a 24-month follow-up if funded. For 100 consenting research participants, aim for at least 80 with 12-month primary outcomes; report actual consented denominators and missing data. Aim for 80% of agreed professional reviews within their clinical window and investigate a 15 percentage-point access/retention gap between priority groups. These are feasibility decision thresholds to agree before enrolment, not demonstrated effects or statistical proof of discrimination.

An independent clinician reviews serious possibly related events and can pause unsafe care immediately. Critical privacy/technology failures require correction and retesting. Every emergency executive decision receives the agreed automatic committee review and appropriate member reporting, protecting individual confidentiality. Zero observed rare harms in 100 people does not establish zero risk.

## Stage 4 — comparative effectiveness and value

The university statistician and research team should design and register a comparison using pilot variance, recruitment and attrition data. Compare A: full Living Well; B: equivalent professional support without the watch/AI package; C: current local services. B/C are research comparators, not Living Well service offers; all Living Well participants receive watch/AI. Also examine the best alternative use of the same private budget. Equal-contact and equal-budget comparisons answer different questions.

Where feasible and ethical, randomise individuals or clusters, account for clustering, assess outcomes independently, analyse as assigned and disclose missingness/crossover. The proposed primary function measure is the Short Physical Performance Battery: balance, walking and chair-rise tasks scored 0–12. A proposed useful mean advantage of 0.5 points at 12 months requires clinical/participant agreement and uncertainty excluding no benefit; pre-specify suitable measures for fitter participants with ceiling effects. Monitor independent living, goals, quality of life, falls, harms and household burden. No invented power calculation or effect is supplied.

## Stage 5 — renew, expand, redesign or stop

Separate decisions about participant benefit, recurring finance, inclusion, governance and net system capacity. Real renewal and affordable full-cost contracts are stronger than positive interviews. A successful startup appeal establishes only the funding it actually provides. Assess outcomes over 12–24 months, role-specific capacity over 24–60 months and durable family effects over 3–5 years with follow-up after 79.

The illustrative decade path 100→250→500→750→1,000 is conditional on gates and remains at 1,000 rather than claiming national demand. Each new location requires workforce, accessible service, member rights and secured finance. If function improves but net capacity is unproved, consider a bounded health proposition with appropriately limited claims. If required technology adds no useful value, return the product scope for a deliberate decision rather than silently removing it.

Pause expansion for unsafe delivery, material inequity, unfunded obligations, uncorrected data failures or no useful comparative benefit. Use funded handover and refund/unused-fund terms to protect participants and donors. The final viability evaluation consolidates the current readiness judgement.
