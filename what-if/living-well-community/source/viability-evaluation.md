# Evaluation of business-case viability

## Current judgement

Develop a properly costed feasibility proposition for Living Well Inc., a new New South Wales (NSW) not-for-profit incorporated association with substantive member decision rights. The association is not established. The proposed university partnership remains unnegotiated. Living Well must itself negotiate and manage every provider, participant, community, technology, funding and evaluation agreement through its committee and paid management.

The case is **not yet demonstrated viable**. No Living Well outcomes, committed underwriting or donations, complete safe-service quotations or legal/privacy clearance exist. Component exercise evidence supports a plausible functional benefit; it does not validate the complete program, AI's added value or wider savings. Confidence in expected benefits is qualitative, not a numerical probability forecast.

## Finance and organisational choice

D1 needs approximately $891,508 gross for the 100-person pilot in 2026 dollars, or $931,925 at assumed 2027–28 prices. D2's corresponding targets are $861,617 and $900,679. Both include $280,500 startup/preparation/evaluation, the full first year and a three-month cash buffer, with assumed 8% gross deductions. D1 is the association base overhead; D2 is a lower-overhead sensitivity for the same association, not a second legal form. The three delivery arrangements have not been separately priced. Recost participatory governance, formation, professional management and university work before any appeal. The $120,000 research allowance is initial/pilot only; comparative and long-term phases require separately secured finance. No commercialisation income is assumed.

Private seed of $66,000 is needed before paid formation/feasibility. Record founders' pre-incorporation obligations and underwriting loss allocation. A successful campaign reimburses approved seed once; failure leaves the agreed risk with the underwriter. No seed is committed. A successful appeal only funds its defined period, not future obligations.

At 250 newly recruited places, the central continuing member/sponsor scenario has annual shortfalls of $181,375 for D1 and $148,375 for D2 in 2026 dollars, including operating reserve but excluding one-time startup. All four recurring funding tests are negative at the D1 midpoint. Continuing-cohort costs can be lower, but later hypothetical surpluses cannot cover earlier shortages or prove affordable demand. Public savings and unpaid contribution are not operator revenue.

NSW guidance positions associations for small-scale non-commercial purposes. Obtain eligibility advice on this fee-based service before incorporation and revisit suitability, registrations and member rights before interstate growth or commercial licensing. A decade expansion scenario is not legal clearance. No automatic change of structure is authorised.

The proposed university agreement must settle funded deliverables, independent publication, ethics/data responsibilities, IP ownership and community use. Failure to agree sustainable terms requires another suitable partner or a revised feasibility decision, not an unsupported promise of research.

## Evidence needed for each decision

| Decision | Evidence before proceeding | If absent |
|---|---|---|
| Form and prepare | Confirm purpose, member/committee rules, name/legal advice, seed liability and funded paid work | Do not incur uncontrolled formation obligations |
| Run crowdfunding | Lawful appeal, realistic donor reach, full quoted budget, truthful claims and refund/unused-fund terms | Redesign the proposition before seeking donations |
| Enrol participants | Cleared usable funds, signed safe delivery/technology contracts, inclusive access, evaluation and exit plan | Delay; never rely on uncollected pledges |
| Renew | Actual needs, costs, affordable payments/contracts and funded continuity | Support handover; do not assume emergency fundraising |
| Commercialise research | Member decision, university/rights-owner agreement, defined contribution and revenue terms, lawful data use and protected community access | No licence or spin-out commitment |
| Expand | Useful comparative outcomes, acceptable harms/equity, paid capacity, real recurring finance and continuity under stress | Narrow claims, redesign or stop expansion |

## Uncertainty and source limits

Measure participant and carer burden, adverse effects, displaced resources and induced medical demand, not just attendance. No robust forecast exists for national savings, longevity or workforce release. Care after 79 and intergenerational effects need funded long follow-up. Governance rights and meetings do not themselves establish resilience.

The supplied taxonomy overview supports governance principles; its linked full 58-decision page returned 404, so no complete row mapping is claimed. The ATO crowdfunding page was not fully retrieved in the preceding check; older clinical/regulator pages were not all refreshed. Current NSW regulator guidance, university course/research pages, NHMRC guidance and a UNSW sample collaboration agreement were checked for this revision. The current NSW legislation endpoint returned 403 and Sydney’s older research-code link returned 404; neither is represented as fully read. Specific legal, clinical, regulatory and tax advice remains required. Charity and deductible-gift status are not automatic consequences of forming a not-for-profit.

The next decision is whether the proposed benefits and management model justify an authorised feasibility phase. This document does not register Living Well, solicit funds, recruit participants or commit resources. The committee must protect current care, privacy and donor restrictions before considering growth.
