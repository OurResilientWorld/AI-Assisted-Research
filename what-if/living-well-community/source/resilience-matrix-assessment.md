# Resilience governance and outcome matrix

## How the programme connects community decisions to outcomes

This programme-specific matrix connects service mechanisms, community decisions and outcomes during disruption. It applies the supplied resilience taxonomy’s governance direction: community agenda setting, decisions, veto and review, with deliberation and automatic review of emergency decisions. The domain labels below organise this proposal’s assessment; no unpublished taxonomy identifiers are assigned.

| Programme domain | Critical community decision | Proposed resilience mechanism | Outcome independent of who governs | Evidence and owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Health and independent function | Which functional goals and inclusive service priorities should the budget support? | Suitable activity and continuing support help preserve daily capability | Independent living, functional tasks, harms and unmet need compared with alternatives | LW-R01; university outcomes lead |
| Family and community relationships | How will the service support freely chosen involvement without demanding unpaid work? | Better function may sustain family contact, care and volunteering | Additional chosen participation, wellbeing and burden after displacement | LW-R02/LW-R05; social/family research leads |
| Care workforce and essential services | Does expansion improve local access after subtracting programme staffing and induced care? | Less compatible care demand may release usable capacity | Role/place/time-specific net capacity, waiting times and essential care maintained under stress | LW-R03; university health-services researcher |
| Household and programme finance | What access policy, crowdfunding target, reserve and renewal terms are acceptable? | Diverse private support and protected cash may maintain service during payer failure | Care continuity, affordable renewal, cash shortfalls and distribution of losses | Finance lead and committee audit oversight and member scrutiny |
| Food, transport and accessible places | Which practical access barriers should contracts and contingency plans address? | Accessible support and local connections may maintain participation during heat/disruption | Missed support, food/transport barriers and recovery time | Access lead; participant/carer reporting |
| Digital, AI, energy and supply | What data use, supplier dependence and offline fallback will members permit? | Technology supports plans, with human continuity if systems fail | Marginal functional value, safe contact, outage recovery and data incidents | LW-R04; technology/privacy leads |
| Governance and emergency action | Who can decide, veto, act urgently and review what happened? | Meaningful member control may improve priorities, trust and accountability | Essential needs met, speed of recovery, total cost and who carried harm | Members, committee and independent governance review |

## Decision rights and emergency practice

Living Well members propose and decide strategic priorities and the budget framework, elect/remove the committee, and decide material expansion and commercialisation after deliberation. The committee checks lawfulness, safety and solvency and oversees execution; paid management handles routine contracts and daily work. This applies the taxonomy’s governance principles without requiring a member vote on each operation. Individual treatment remains confidential and clinically governed. During an incident, managers use delegated continuity powers and the clinical lead can stop unsafe activity. A proposed committee review within 30 days records reasons, outcomes and corrective action, followed by an appropriate member report.

Governance assistance from AI is advisory: explain evidence and uncertainty, show alternative interpretations and help deliberation. Decisions remain with accountable people. Access to the evidence cannot depend on technical skill or willingness to share personal health information.

## Testing the governance hypothesis

Compare participation breadth, whose proposals reach the agenda, whether member input changes decisions, use of constitutional protections/review, responsiveness and outcomes. Record refusal, domination, conflict and burdens as well as participation. A meeting, vote or successful appeal is an output, not proof of resilience. Community control could improve responsiveness or could favour vocal/wealthy members and slow necessary action. Test those competing explanations against service continuity, outcomes, costs and the distribution of loss.

## Source boundary

The supplied *Who Decides? A Map of the Decisions That Will Make or Break Resilience*, v 0.3.1 dated 16November 2026, describes 58 decisions across 13 systems and the four governance powers. The linked full taxonomy returned 404 during retrieval; the supplied overview does not contain all 58 definitions, so full taxonomy coverage and exact row identifiers cannot be verified. This matrix is the programme’s application of the governance ideas, not a reconstruction or adoption of missing rows. The overall evidence judgement is in the final viability evaluation.

The university research partnership is responsible for the agreed evaluation work across these measures; qualified service providers retain individual clinical responsibility. Research phases, independence and long-term funding follow the [partnership proposal](university-research-partnership.md).
