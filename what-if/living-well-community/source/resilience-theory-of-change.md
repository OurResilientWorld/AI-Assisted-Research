# How the service could support resilience

## Proposed mechanism

```text
People identify needs; Living Well members elect and scrutinise the committee
    → new Living Well organisation, paid management and fully funded startup campaign
    → accessible professional support + tailored exercise/nutrition + watch/AI
    → sustained engagement and functional ability [LW-R01]
    → chosen additional family/community participation [LW-R02]
    → less compatible care demand, minus programme and induced care [LW-R03]
    → essential household support and local care maintained during disruption

Technology must add useful value above equivalent human support [LW-R04].
Family benefits must persist without transferring unwanted burdens [LW-R05].
```

Each arrow requires evidence. Exercise component research supports a plausible starting point, while local governance and funding must demonstrate their own contribution. Benefits cannot be inferred from device use, a fundraising target or a meeting attendance count.

| Link | Evidence to collect | Competing explanation or harm |
|---|---|---|
| Community authority → priorities | Which proposals changed budgets/access; whose views were absent | Vocal or wealthy members dominate; consultation is mistaken for control |
| Crowdfunding → dependable service | Net usable funds, restrictions, obligations and continuity | A popular campaign overpromises; funds arrive late or cannot pay core bills |
| Support → functional gain | Comparative function and participant goals including withdrawals | Volunteers would improve anyway; extra contact explains gains |
| Function → chosen contribution | Additional mutually exclusive time diaries and wellbeing | Activity displaces another volunteer or becomes unwanted work |
| Function → care capacity | Compatible avoided hours minus programme/induced demand | New diagnoses add care; saved time cannot be redeployed |
| Capacity → shock performance | Contact, essential support, recovery and loss distribution | Programme fails alongside the services it depends on |
| Short-term benefit → family gain | Follow-up, carer choice, costs and outcomes beyond 79 | Benefits decay or younger relatives inherit greater later burden |

## Delivery arrangements and risk

Living Well is proposed as a New South Wales (NSW) not-for-profit incorporated association. Members set priorities and the budget framework, elect/remove the committee and decide material expansion and commercialisation after deliberation. The committee manages lawfully through paid staff. Test whether accessible participation actually distributes influence and whether legal/clinical safeguards work. D1/D2 are base/lower overhead scenarios for this same association, not different governance forms. University researchers lead initial and longitudinal evaluation independently; university involvement does not itself establish resilience.

Crowdfunding can broaden support or bias resources toward visible stories and prosperous areas. A committee must allocate care by agreed needs/access criteria, not individual popularity. No donor can purchase treatment priority, personal data or extra votes. Document opportunity costs: the same private resources could fund existing exercise, nutrition or practical support services.

Compare those alternatives on outcomes and total resources. An improvement in health may justify a health-service decision even if net care capacity or national resilience is not demonstrated; claims and expansion criteria must then be narrowed explicitly.
