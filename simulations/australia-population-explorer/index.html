What does “physically here” mean?

Imagine counting everyone in Australia on a particular date: people who live here, temporary residents and visiting travellers. Count each person once.

Someone who lives in Australia but is overseas that day is not physically here. Someone visiting Australia that day is.

The historical estimates are June snapshots, not the total number of visitors over a whole year. Physical-presence data is missing for June 2000–2006. The orange line shows residents for those years instead, clearly labelled. Residents can include people away overseas and exclude short-term visitors.

    How the calculation works

    Each year, residents change through births, deaths, arrivals and departures. The model ages people using Australian fertility and survival data. People physically here = residents + non-resident visitors here − residents overseas.

    Arrivals and departures are the annual numbers you choose. Protection granted to people already in Australia lets them stay; granting the visa itself does not add another person. People may leave on a different visa from the one they arrived on.

    All visa groups use the observed overall migrant age pattern because separate age patterns for each visa group are not available here. Source rounding residuals are retained. Visitor snapshots are not annual trip totals, visitor visa stocks or housing demand.

    How to use this comparison

    “Cut visas” can mean different things. Giving fewer new visas may reduce future arrivals. Having fewer people holding temporary visas could also mean people leave, or move to another visa while staying in Australia. Those are different changes.

    For example, if a student already here moves to a permanent visa, there is one fewer temporary visa holder but no fewer people in Australia. That is why the promise of 750,000 fewer temporary visas cannot simply be entered as 750,000 people leaving.

    Start with the category table. In Screen 2, choose a group and test fewer arrivals or more departures. Or choose a P2/P3 experiment to test the missing future totals. Your experiment changes your line in Screen 2; the comparison here stays fixed so you can always return to it.
