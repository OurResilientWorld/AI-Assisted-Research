Australia population explorer

AI-Assisted Research · Our Resilient World
By Rick Molony · Developed with AI assistance.

Open the explorer

Explore how Australia’s population could change under different migration, birth, mortality and travel assumptions. The dashboard places historical evidence, conditional futures and editable assumptions together. It does not predict a single future or recommend a political party.

Start with the chart

The default measure is People physically here: residents plus visitors temporarily in Australia, minus residents temporarily overseas. Resident population and the share aged 65 or older are alternative measures.

History occupies the left panel. Conditional futures occupy the slightly wider right panel. Both use the same vertical scale; the vertical axis does not start at zero. Years have different horizontal spacing between panels, so compare their values rather than their apparent slopes. The narrow shaded gap separates the last observation from the 2026 model anchor. No missing observations are filled in.

Only the Labor and One Nation future curves are dashed. The two dotted vertical boundaries mark the source/model gap; they are not pathways. The chart key directly below the chart lets you show or hide the fixed references. A solid vertical marker identifies the inspected future year. If two scenarios coincide, their curves overlap; the numbers and comparison selector make the difference explicit.

The Coalition is marked Not quantified because the pinned evidence does not define a numerical pathway. This does not mean zero migration or that the party has no policy. Select it to read the explanation.

Explore a possible future

  1. Use Start from to choose migration categories, historical BAU, Labor or One Nation. Starting again replaces your editable assumptions after confirmation.
  2. Select the years to which an edit applies. The default is 2027–2045. Sliders and exact-value boxes show the first selected year; a label identifies differing annual values.
  3. Change a main slider and watch the chart and outcome cards respond. Use Details or All inputs & annual detail for departures, other categories, age assumptions, delayed departures, travel and mortality.
  4. Use Compare with to choose a fixed reference for the numerical differences. It does not replace your scenario. Changing your assumptions never changes a fixed reference.
  5. Use the future-year slider, or the 2035/2045 buttons, to inspect outcomes. View the numbers opens annual results and the category-flow breakdown beside the chart.

Policy-total modes supply net migration directly. They do not invent arrivals, departures or category allocations. Their migration controls change the specifically labelled assumption years, independently of the general year range. Choose migration categories when you want to model those individual flows.

Undo restores the previous valid assumptions. Reset Chart confirms before clearing your personal changes and returning the actual calculation and comparison to Historical BAU. The starting and reset views are identical: observed history, the thick black BAU · projected future line, and the dashed Labor and One Nation pathways. History and projected BAU have separate keys. The measure returns to People physically here, the inspected year to 2045, and the edit interval to 2027–2045. Both party comparisons are checked; category starting settings are not a chart pathway.

The green personal curve is hidden while the calculation is unmodified BAU. It reappears when you change an assumption or choose another starting scenario. To vary migration categories after resetting, choose Migration categories under Start from; BAU itself uses a fixed historical migration rate. Undo recovers previous assumptions after a reset; it does not rewind chart-view choices. Saving and loading an unmodified BAU scenario also keeps the redundant green curve hidden. First opening the app uses exactly the same BAU inputs and chart view as Reset Chart. Older saved files remain loadable; a previously saved category-start chart line is omitted, while its numerical comparison and model inputs remain supported.

Understand what changed

Outcome cards distinguish residents, older residents, physical presence and annual net migration. The comparison explanation reports the selected measure. A separate line attributes the resident-population effect of your edits against the unedited starting mode. A difference from BAU alone does not prove that you edited anything: category starting settings and historical BAU are different scenarios.

Review changes lists changed assumptions. Sources & interpretation, immediately after it, explains the reference pathways, evidence and measurement definitions. Method & limitations is in the footer.

In detailed annual results, 2026 is an initial modelled snapshot: annual flows are shown as unavailable, not zero observations. Age groups are 0–19, 20–64 and 65+. Onshore protection grants change the status of people already here and are not counted as new arrivals. Visitors and residents abroad are stocks at June, not annual flow totals.

Explore the history

Choose Explore the history. Select a June year with the menu, or click/drag within the historical chart while the history panel is open. The menu provides the same selection without a pointer. Choose a government period to total overlapping financial years, or Show all periods to return to the full history.

The panel includes population-change components, annual net migration and changes from the preceding year, migration categories, and refugee/humanitarian grants. Expand the sections you need. Historical selection does not alter your future assumptions.

Government changes can occur within a financial year. Mixed years are labelled and can appear in both adjacent period totals: do not add those totals together. Timing alone does not establish that a government caused a result. COVID-related changes have specific explanations where ordinary percentage comparisons would mislead.

Category history begins in 2004–05. Earlier category data is unavailable. A dash in the grants table means source N/A, not zero. Visa grants, border movements, population stocks and unique people are different measures. The category balance divided by arrivals is not an individual retention rate. Selected history and grants can be saved as CSV.

Read political proposals in context

Policy detail includes fixed scenario results, annual net-migration assumptions, and written category proposals with their source links. The source-policy review date is 21 September 2026; the dataset is a pinned snapshot and is not automatically updated.

Labor’s 2027 and 2028 net-migration inputs are 245,000 and 225,000; later years use an explicit model assumption. One Nation’s first three negative years use an illustrative magnitude of −100,000 annually, followed by 130,000. The negative magnitude, annual ceiling attainment and the model’s start timing are assumptions, not a complete party-announced schedule.

Explore this category starts a fresh category experiment after confirmation. It does not turn an unquantified written proposal into a numerical party promise. Save your current experiment first if you want to keep both.

Save, return and explain

The header has three actions: Undo, Reset Chart and Save & share. Open Save & share for:

  • Save scenario: downloads a JSON file with the input schedule, model/source identity, comparison reference, measure, inspected year and visible reference lines.
  • Load saved scenario: confirms before replacing your inputs and restoring that saved view. Compatible input-only files also work; select their view separately.
  • Copy or save an explanation: optional editable text describing the comparison and its assumptions. Give the recipient the scenario file too so they can reproduce it.

Nothing is posted automatically. The file is downloaded to your browser’s usual download location; it is not a cloud account or a public share link. Keep it somewhere you can find again. A retry action in the panel can download the most recently prepared file again. The application cannot verify that you retained a download. Unsaved edits do not persist after closing or reloading the page.

Invalid input leaves the last valid chart visible and pauses saves, exports and printing until you restore valid inputs. An invalid scenario file is rejected before replacing the valid experiment. Loading a valid file can also recover the experiment. The file includes its model and data identity; an incompatible file is rejected rather than silently reinterpreted.

CSV exports contain results or selected evidence tables; they do not replace the full scenario file. Print current view prints the overview and, if open, expands the current detail panel for printing. Choose your browser’s PDF destination if you want a PDF. Return to Controls first to print only the overview.

Limits and access

Use the keyboard to reach buttons, menus and exact-value inputs. Arrow keys adjust a focused slider. Details open beside the main chart on a wide screen and below it on a narrow screen. A bottom summary on narrow screens helps you return to the chart. Wide evidence tables scroll within their panel; focus their scroll area to navigate with the keyboard.

The model does not estimate housing prices, employment, economic benefits or an individual’s visa outcome. Optional delayed departures, retention, duration and category age shifts are sensitivity assumptions, not measured individual behaviour. Historical fit does not establish predictive validity. Read the source notes before drawing or sharing a conclusion.

The application calculates locally after the page is loaded. Internet access is needed only when you choose an external source or website link. JavaScript is required for calculation. Model 0.4.1 and scenario schema 2 identify the calculation inputs; the source fingerprint is available under Method & limitations.
