Australia population explorer — user guide

Version 4.2 · Updated 19 October 2026
AI-Assisted Research · Our Resilient World
By Rick Molony · Developed with AI assistance.

Open the explorer · All simulations

Compare four possible futures, change migration assumptions and inspect the historical evidence. The explorer shows conditional experiments, not forecasts, party endorsements or estimates of what a policy is certain to achieve.

The first tab is Projections. History follows it. Switching between them preserves your projection settings for the current visit.

Try an experiment in two minutes

  1. Open Projections. BAU is the initial starting future, with no edits. Your teal line overlaps BAU. Both charts initially inspect 2045; the population measure is Resident population.
  2. Under Students, move Arrivals to +5%. This adds about 7,836 arrivals per year at full effect, using observed 2024–25 student arrivals as the scale. The population and annual net-migration charts respond.
  3. Use Undo, then Redo, to compare the change. The four fixed reference futures do not move.
  4. Open the Students Policy & controls button. Read the party statements beside the related sliders. A family-arrivals reduction is your assumed effect; it is not a measured party allocation.
  5. Open Numbers & changes below the charts to inspect the annual accounting and your settings. Use Save scenario ↓ to keep a file that reproduces your experiment.
  6. Open History, choose a year and a migration category, then return to Projections. Your edits remain in place.

Compare four futures

The cards show resident population in the inspected year. Select Use Labor, Use Coalition, Use One Nation or Use BAU to choose your starting future. Choosing another base retains your category and other edits; it does not reset the experiment. Reset restores the initial BAU settings, resident-population chart and 2045 inspection year. Reset takes effect immediately and can be undone.

Fixed future What it represents
BAU — slate A continuation of the pinned historical net-migration rate. Your unedited scenario reproduces its annual schedule.
Labor — purple 245,000 net migrants in 2027 and 225,000 in 2028; 225,000 thereafter is an explicit continuation assumption.
Coalition — blue The Liberal–National proposal released on 6 October 2026: 100,000 in 2028–29, 100,000 in 2029–30, 130,000 in 2030–31 and 160,000 in 2031–32.
One Nation — orange Three initially negative years, modelled as 2027–2029 at an illustrative −100,000 a year, followed by 130,000 a year. The negative annual amount and model start timing are assumptions.
Your scenario — dashed teal Your selected base plus your edits. The fixed party lines remain unchanged.

Coalition financial years map to the year in which they end: 2029, 2030, 2031 and 2032. The model uses the existing Labor numbers for 2027–2028 as a bridging assumption and holds 160,000 from 2033–2045 as a continuation assumption. Neither is an announced Coalition annual schedule. No housing-completions feedback is modelled.

Labor and One Nation use the retained research snapshot checked on 21 September 2026. Coalition uses the 6 October release, checked on 18 October 2026. These are dated snapshots, not a feed of the latest announcements. Open Future assumptions & totals to inspect the evidence and adjust the numerical assumptions for your selected base.

Read the charts and numbers

The first chart’s title is a button. Choose residents, people physically here, an age group, births, deaths, the share aged 65+, or the age ratio. The second chart always shows Annual net migration: arrivals minus departures. Negative net migration means more departures than arrivals.

The Inspect slider selects a year from 2026 to 2045. It changes which year you read, not when your edits apply. The 2026 point is a model anchor, not an observed annual flow. Annual future flows begin in 2027; the annual results table shows dashes for the anchor’s births, deaths and migration.

Population charts may use a vertical axis above zero. Read the labels and numbers rather than judging the line’s visual steepness alone. Overlapping lines can be identical. The teal result card reports the resident-population difference from the unedited base in the selected year.

People physically here = residents + non-resident visitors here − residents temporarily overseas. Visitors and residents abroad are stocks at June; they are not annual immigration or emigration totals.

The age ratio is people aged 65+ per 100 aged 20–64. It is not an employment, fiscal-cost or economic-burden estimate. Age 85+ is a subset of 65+.

Use the migration levers

All eleven broad categories have separate Arrivals and Departures sliders and exact-value boxes. The percentage is a change relative to that category’s observed 2024–25 annual flow. It is not the share of total migration or the percentage change in the final population.

Category Important distinction
Students Accompanying family is not separately identified in the retained historical series.
Working holidays Visa stocks and program eligibility are not annual resident arrivals.
Visitors counted as residents These movements differ from short-term visitors physically present at June.
Other temporary · including graduates The series combines groups; it does not isolate graduate movements.
Temporary skilled Separate from permanent skilled and permanent family reunion.
Permanent skilled Accompanying-family assumptions sit inside this category.
Permanent family Not the same as family accompanying students or skilled workers.
Humanitarian & special eligibility A movement category, distinct from humanitarian/protection visa grants.
Australian citizens Includes resident arrivals and departures by Australian citizens.
New Zealand citizens A separate observed movement category.
Other permanent Remaining permanent movements in the retained category definition.

Policy-discussed categories appear first. Within that group, and then among the remaining categories, order follows the larger annual effect of a 5% change in observed arrivals or departures. This indicates flow size, not political importance or the feasibility of changing it. Visa compliance is separately labelled because no comparable measured party-effect baseline is supplied.

Your annual net migration is:

Selected future + category arrival changes − category departure changes + additional total adjustment − extra compliance departures.

The research does not provide complete party-specific category allocations. Category edits are therefore additional experiments on top of your selected future, not a breakdown of that party’s announced total. Do not enter the same reduction both as a category edit and as another policy adjustment.

Policy & controls (or Details & controls) opens the category’s sliders and explanation together. Related controls include student-family and skilled-family arrivals avoided, graduate arrivals avoided and extra resident-visitor departures. Visa compliance supplies another explicit departure assumption. These controls start at zero. Announced visa stocks, places or caps are not automatically converted into annual migrants.

A detailed category can be reset with Reset this category. This restores its arrival/departure/age edits and its associated policy reductions; it does not reset every setting. ← All migration levers or Escape returns to the grid.

Set timing and secondary assumptions

Open Timing: 2027–2045 to change the intervention interval. Start changes in and Last year with changed settings are inclusive. Years to reach full change controls the ramp: zero is immediate; a four-year ramp applies 25%, 50%, 75%, then 100%.

After the edit period, annual input settings return to the selected base; people already added can still affect later population. If you enable Model later departures after changed temporary arrivals, those changed arrivals generate a later departure response using your chosen duration and share staying longer, including after the edit period. This is an illustrative response rule, not an estimated visa-holder stock or a measured retention rate.

Future assumptions & totals contains an additional annual net-migration adjustment and the selected party’s editable total assumptions. They apply within the same intervention period. Editing a party assumption changes your teal scenario, not its fixed reference line.

Other assumptions contains:

  • Children per woman: 1.35–1.65 around a starting value of 1.5.
  • Age-specific death risks: 0.90–1.10 times baseline. This is an annual risk level, not a compounded improvement rate.
  • Migrant age-profile shifts: sensitivities using shared profiles, not calibrated category-specific ages.
  • Non-resident visitors physically here and Residents temporarily overseas: June stocks affecting physical presence.
  • Onshore protection grants: a status count for people already here; changing it does not add population or migration.

The fertility and mortality bounds are exploration limits, not estimates of what government policy can achieve. Changing them does not silently recalculate your base migration schedule.

Explore History

The second tab is History. Its year slider spans 2000–2025. The cards show values for the selected year; the charts retain their available historical series.

Data Available years ending June
Resident population in the population-component series 2000–2024
Births, deaths and net migration in that series 2001–2024; 2000 is a starting snapshot
Eleven migration categories, plus unknown visas/rounding 2005–2025, meaning financial years 2004–05 to 2024–25
Physical presence, visitors here and residents abroad 2007–2025
Humanitarian and protection grants 1978–2025; some fields are not applicable

Choose a category card to see its annual arrivals, departures and net balance. Their order matches Projections. Unknown visas / rounding is an extra historical reconciliation category, not an additional projection lever.

Expand Population numbers & download, Category numbers & download, Presence numbers & download or All grant numbers & download for tables and CSVs. Download all category history exports every available category-year row.

A dash means unavailable or not applicable, never zero. Before 2005 there is no category history here. The population-component series stops in 2024; a separate presence series includes 2025 residents. The explorer does not silently splice the two or substitute residents for missing physical presence.

Data coverage & gaps explains which finer policy effects cannot be measured from these sources. Student and skilled dependants, graduate-only movements, compliance effects, duration and retention lack separate histories in the retained dataset. Birth/death totals do not supply annual fertility or age-specific mortality rates. Missing here does not mean that no such statistics exist elsewhere.

Arrival and departure categories describe visa status at movement, not matched individuals: their ratio is not a retention rate. Grants are not additional arrivals. Historical changes do not establish what a particular government caused.

History selection is browsing state. It does not change your future inputs and is not included in saved projection files. Reset restores the projection experiment; it does not reset the separate History selection.

Save, load, export and recover

  • Save scenario ↓ downloads australia-population-v4-scenario.json: your base, all projection settings, timing, inspected future year, chart measure and model/data identity.
  • Load replaces the current projection settings with a compatible saved file. Undo can restore the preceding scenario.
  • Numbers & changes lists annual accounting and changed settings. It exports all five paths, or annual category changes. The footer’s Download results also exports all five paths.
  • Download policy comparison exports the source-labelled written policy evidence. History has its own data exports.
  • Use the browser’s print command to print the active view or save a PDF. Scenario JSON is the reproducible settings file; a PDF or CSV does not replace it.

Calculations and downloads run locally after the page loads. There is no account, automatic upload or cloud save. Keep your downloaded file: unsaved edits are lost on reload or closing the page. Undo/Redo history is not included in a saved scenario.

Blank, out-of-range or incompatible entries do not replace the last valid scenario. A numerical field can show invalid text until corrected; the charts and saved/exported results still use the last valid committed values. Impossible combined reductions are rejected with an explanation. Reduce overlapping reductions or change the delayed-departure assumption. Invalid files leave the current experiment intact.

Earlier saved files

V4.2 accepts compatible V4 scenario files, including original V4 schema-1 files. Those retain their existing settings and results; inactive Coalition defaults are added. New saves use V4 schema 2.

V3 and earlier scenario files are not imported or converted by V4.2. Their schema numbers have different meanings. Use the original simulator for those files. The earlier public V3 file is retained on GitHub: download its raw index.html, open the downloaded file locally, and use that version’s load action. Do not rename a saved file’s version fields to force an import.

Keyboard access and limitations

Use Tab to reach buttons, links, sliders and number boxes. Arrow keys adjust a focused slider. Enter commits a number-box edit. On the Projections/History tabs, Left/Right changes tab; Home selects Projections and End selects History. Escape closes a projection detail view. History table regions can receive keyboard focus for scrolling. On smaller screens, cards and charts stack vertically; wide tables scroll within their region.

JavaScript is required. Charts, controls and embedded historical data need no external runtime download once the HTML has loaded. Source links require internet access.

The retained cohort model has common age profiles and simplified response assumptions. It does not estimate housing prices, employment, fiscal effects, regional settlement or an individual’s visa outcome. Independent demographic calibration, hindcasting, category dynamics and representative citizen validation remain outstanding. Passing implementation tests is not proof of predictive validity.

The interaction design draws on En-ROADS; this explorer is separate and does not claim affiliation or equivalent scientific validation.

Sources and terms

Historical data are retained research extracts, not automatically refreshed series. The explorer links original sources beside the relevant evidence, including:

  • ABS national population.
  • ABS Overseas Migration 2024–25.
  • ABS population physically present.
  • Home Affairs historical migration statistics.
  • Coalition announcement, 6 October 2026.
