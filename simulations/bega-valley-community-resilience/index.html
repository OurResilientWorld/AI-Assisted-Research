Inspect the evidence, model and limits

A trustworthy simulation makes it easy to disagree with its assumptions and update them.

Evidence used to choose the six issues
  • BVSC Community Satisfaction Survey 2024 — formal random sample plus separate opt-in results.
  • Community Strategic Plan 2042, 2025 revision — published engagement synthesis.
  • 2026 draft plans and budget consultation — indicative public digital questions.
  • 2026 clinical services consultation — active health planning, no results inferred.
  • Merimbula CBD consultation and Cuttagee Bridge campaign — concrete current trade-offs, not representative samples.
  • Rick Molony's 2020 Royal Commission submission — lived evidence about Bega Valley evacuations, information, communications, power and road dependencies.
How the directional indicators are calculated

In plain English: the demo first looks at how many points you gave each action. The first points make a bigger difference to the display than later points, so concentrating 40 points in one area does not make it twice as influential as 20. The table then states how strongly this demo assumes each action supports each of the five overall goals. The coloured scores combine those two things. They are discussion prompts—not predictions that services or resilience will improve.

Each action's points receive a diminishing-returns transform: min(1, sqrt(points / 25)). Each indicator is the weighted average of the six transformed values multiplied by 100.

What a weight means: a larger number means this demo assumes a stronger connection. For example, 0.35 is treated as stronger than 0.10. It does not mean a 35% improvement in the real world. The weights in each goal column add to 1.00 so the model can divide that goal across the six actions.

These weights are starting assumptions for discussion, not survey findings. Changing a weight changes the coloured bars and map lines only. It does not change what people said, prove that one action causes an outcome, or predict what a policy will achieve.

Known limitations and stop conditions
  • The scores show how this demonstration responds to your choices. They do not predict what will happen in Bega Valley.
  • The 100 effort points are a conversation device. They are not money, votes, measured benefits or proof that an action can be delivered.
  • The published results of council's 2026 survey were not available when this demonstration was prepared, so they are not represented.
  • Online comments and campaigns show that an issue is being discussed, but they cannot tell us how many residents agree or whether the wider community shares that view.
  • The six action areas simplify a connected system and may leave out important community priorities. The “missing issue” question is intended to expose those gaps.
  • This is a browser demonstration only. It has no secure database, identity checks, moderation, long-term comparison or automatic submission to representatives.
  • Stop and seek review if a response contains personal information, invented responses are no longer clearly labelled, a score is described as a fact or prediction, or anyone proposes publishing or sending results without separate approval.
