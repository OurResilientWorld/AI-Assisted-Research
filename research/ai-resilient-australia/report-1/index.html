Report 1 · The significance test

What Would Make Australia Resilient by 2035 — and the Risks That Threaten It

  • Author: Rick Molony
  • Developed with AI assistance
  • Evidence to 22 July 2026
  • CC BY 4.0

Are Australia's major resilience risks severe and connected enough that current governance is not keeping pace?

H1 verdict

SUPPORTED

Australia faces an interacting risk system across trade, supply, defence, governance, care, technological adaptation and social cohesion. The management gap lies not only inside each pillar but between institutions and shocks.

What the evidence shows

Seven pillars remain distinct

Trade and earn; feed, power and supply; defend and ally; govern and adapt; care for people; adapt to technology; and hold together each have different evidence and response needs.

Interaction is the decisive issue

Trade, budget, workforce, supply-chain, disaster, health and trust pressures can cascade and compete for the same reserves.

Several pressures are already visible

Official records show ageing, productivity and fiscal pressure, modelled health-workforce gaps, compounding climate risk and unfinished biosecurity reform.

Strong pieces are not yet a bridge

Australia has capable institutions and assets, but the accepted finding is that they are not yet connected and load-bearing at the required pace and scale.

What this verdict does not mean

  • Australia is weak or incapable
  • every projection is a precise 2035 forecast
  • one distant figure carries the verdict
  • AI is proved necessary by the risk diagnosis
  • conventional resilience measures are unnecessary

Where this argument could be wrong

The interaction finding would weaken if several major pillar pressures improve materially, if institutions demonstrate effective cross-system coordination, or if the claimed cascade channels prove less important than the accepted evidence indicates.

Bottom line

The significance claim holds. Australia's risks are serious enough and connected enough to test whether new tools are needed, but Report 1 does not establish that AI is the answer.
