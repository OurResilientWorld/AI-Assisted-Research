Case study 02 · Research series

Australia's AI resilience and readiness to 2035

Does Australia need AI to manage its 2035 resilience risks, and can it use AI well enough to justify the risks?

What is being tested: AI is justified only if it measurably strengthens resilience under disciplined governance, public legitimacy and institutional readiness.

  • Evidence available to 22 July 2026
  • Three reports
  • Australia

Cumulative series result

CONDITIONALLY SUPPORTED

Australia needs AI for some 2035 resilience risks, but the benefit is useful only if governance capacity is built and demonstrated in time.

What this does not mean: AI is not sufficient, not every resilience problem is technological, and an announced policy is not a delivered control.

Report extracts

Read the tests in sequence

Report 1 — What Would Make Australia Resilient by 2035 — and the Risks That Threaten It

Australia faces an interacting risk system across trade, supply, defence, governance, care, technological adaptation and social cohesion. The management gap lies not only inside each pillar but between institutions and shocks.

Read Report 1 extract

Report 2 — Do the Benefits Outweigh the Costs?

AI-led transformation can materially help on some resilience risks and can sometimes change the premise of a problem. The benefit is uneven, information integrity worsens, and the net result depends on transformation pace and effective governance.

Read Report 2 extract

Report 3 — Can Australia Actually Do It?

Australia's capacity is partial and uneven. The July 2026 policy shift improves the stated direction, but no condition is clearly adequate for broad-to-deep transformation and delivery remains untested.

Read Report 3 extract

Evidence discipline

Reading controls

  • The seven resilience pillars remain distinct even though their pressures interact.
  • T1–T4 are scenarios and structured judgements, not forecasts or observed probabilities.
  • The projected 2035 nursing gap is a modelled workforce gap, not a current observed gap.
  • The 20–30 per cent task-hours case is illustrative, not a forecast.
  • The July 2026 policy shift changes stated direction; delivery remains untested.

Retest rule: New evidence changes the published result only through documented evidence review and Product Owner acceptance.
