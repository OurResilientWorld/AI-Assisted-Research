Report 3 · AI governance, state capacity and the race to 2035

Can Australia Actually Do It?

  • Author: Rick Molony
  • Developed with AI assistance
  • Evidence to 22 July 2026
  • CC BY 4.0

Can Australia build and demonstrate governance capacity quickly enough for AI's contribution to improve resilience without transformation outrunning control?

H3 verdict

NOT YET / CONDITIONAL

Australia's capacity is partial and uneven. The July 2026 policy shift improves the stated direction, but no condition is clearly adequate for broad-to-deep transformation and delivery remains untested.

What the evidence shows

Pace governance

Inadequate but newly improving in stated direction; delivery untested.

Functional accountability

Relevant reforms and institutions exist, but protection at scale is not yet demonstrated.

Sovereignty

Weak but improving; a temporary foreign access restriction demonstrated dependence while binding continuity controls remain uncertain.

Benefit steering

Largely aspirational, with few binding levers or demonstrated distributional outcomes.

Information integrity

Federal capacity remains inadequate despite genuine but jurisdictionally limited state measures.

The cumulative result

H1 is supported, H2 conditionally supported and H3 not yet; therefore H0 remains conditionally supported rather than becoming an unconditional claim.

What this verdict does not mean

  • Australia has no relevant laws, institutions or capability
  • the governance gap cannot close before 2035
  • announced standards are already enacted or enforced
  • foreign comparators have solved AI governance
  • AI is necessary for every resilience pillar

Where this argument could be wrong

The judgement could improve if standards become binding and independently evaluated, automated-decision safeguards prevent comparable failures, sovereignty obligations become operational, benefits are measurably shared and electoral authorities gain effective tools. Direction alone is not the retest; demonstrated delivery is.

Bottom line

Australia needs AI for some 2035 resilience risks, but must still prove it can build and enforce the capacity to govern that AI fast enough for the benefit to be safely realised.
