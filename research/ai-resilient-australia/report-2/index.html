Report 2 · AI, transformation and Australia's 2035 risks

Do the Benefits Outweigh the Costs?

  • Author: Rick Molony
  • Developed with AI assistance
  • Evidence to 22 July 2026
  • CC BY 4.0

Can AI-led transformation materially improve resilience after the costs, uneven effects and transformation pace are counted?

H2 verdict

CONDITIONALLY SUPPORTED

AI-led transformation can materially help on some resilience risks and can sometimes change the premise of a problem. The benefit is uneven, information integrity worsens, and the net result depends on transformation pace and effective governance.

What the evidence shows

Four pathways test pace rather than predict it

T1 Plateau, T2 Broad diffusion, T3 Deep transformation and T4 Discontinuity expose how the result changes. They are scenarios and structured judgements, not forecasts.

The potential benefit differs by pillar

The strongest cases involve care and workforce augmentation, disaster and biosecurity capability, and some supply-chain resilience. AI does little to remove core trade exposure.

Costs scale with transformation

Automation error, labour churn, foreign dependence, energy demand, unreliability, concentration and opportunity cost can all grow as deployment deepens.

Governance is the binding condition

The best outcome is broad-to-deep transformation under effective governance; fast transformation under status-quo governance is the trap.

Five conditions control the result

Pace must be governable, accountability functional, sovereignty a deployment condition, benefit steered, and information integrity ring-fenced.

What this verdict does not mean

  • faster transformation is automatically better
  • AI removes Australia's trade or geopolitical exposure
  • the T1–T4 pathways are observed probabilities
  • a useful application is automatically a public-interest gain
  • Australia has already demonstrated effective governance

Where this argument could be wrong

The conditional benefit could weaken if operational trials fail, scale costs rise faster than expected or the transformation pathways overstate feasible deployment. It could strengthen if safe, independently evaluated Australian outcomes demonstrate durable resilience gains.

Bottom line

Pace without governance is the trap; governance without pace can leave the resilience prize unclaimed. Report 2 supports a material but conditional contribution, not an unconditional technology endorsement.
