Report 2 extract — Net benefit and conditionality (H2/H3)

Can new job creation outweigh displacement, and what can support prove?

  • Author: Rick Molony
  • Developed with AI assistance
  • Evidence to 21 July 2026
  • CC BY 4.0

Report 2 tests whether AI-enabled creation can be ranked above displacement and augmentation, and whether targeted support is necessary and sufficient to make creation broad, inclusive and higher quality.

H2 — net benefit

NOT SUPPORTED ON CURRENT EVIDENCE

Current evidence does not provide a common Australian measure of jobs displaced, jobs augmented, viable businesses created, earnings, survival or distribution. Creation cannot be ranked first on the available record.

H3-N — necessity

PARTIALLY SUPPORTED

Recurring barriers support targeted help as likely important for broad access, but the strict counterfactual that broad access would not occur without it has not been tested.

H3-S — sufficiency

UNRESOLVED

Programme announcements and policy instruments do not show that support makes creation dominant or produces viable, secure and broadly accessible work.

What the evidence shows

Three effects, no common measure

Displacement, augmentation and creation can occur together. Available Australian evidence measures them differently, so it cannot yet rank creation as the largest effect.

Augmentation is a competing force

Australian official analysis emphasises upskilling, redeployment and role evolution. International job-ad evidence also points to rapid skill change in AI-exposed work.

Barriers make targeted support relevant

Small firms report gaps in skills, time, security, finance and implementation. Government and intergovernmental plans respond to those barriers, supporting the case for testing targeted help.

Policy instruments are not outcomes

A programme can exist and improve access without proving that creation becomes the largest labour-market effect or that the resulting work provides viable income, survival and protection.

What these verdicts do not mean

  • AI-enabled creation is small or absent
  • displacement is proven to exceed creation
  • targeted support is unnecessary
  • existing support is sufficient to produce dominant, higher-quality creation
  • all ages, regions and occupations face the same effects

Where this argument could be wrong

The H2 verdict could be too conservative if official statistics miss substantial viable new businesses, independent work and employee-to-owner transitions. The necessity finding could be too strong if inexpensive tools diffuse broadly without targeted intervention. Sufficiency could clear if a well-evaluated programme demonstrates viable work at scale.

Bottom line

Creation cannot yet be ranked above displacement and augmentation. Evidence partly supports targeted help as important for broad access, but neither strict necessity nor sufficiency is demonstrated. Support should therefore be treated as a test with pre-declared outcomes, not as proof that the hypothesis is true.
