Report 3 extract — Shock adequacy (H4) and cumulative verdict

Can new job creation remain viable through a major shock?

  • Author: Rick Molony
  • Developed with AI assistance
  • Evidence to 21 July 2026
  • CC BY 4.0

Report 3 asks whether the creation pathway and its support remain measurable and actionable through a major enduring shock, then combines all five tests into the series verdict.

H4 — shock adequacy

SCENARIO JUDGEMENT — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED AS A NECESSARY CONDITION, NOT EMPIRICALLY DEMONSTRATED

Current risk and supply-chain evidence makes stress testing necessary, but Australia has not experienced a qualifying shock with an evaluated AI-creation programme.

Cumulative series result

NOT SUPPORTED ON CURRENT EVIDENCE

H1 passes only as an existence threshold. H2 does not pass, H3 necessity is partial, H3 sufficiency is unresolved and H4 is not empirically demonstrated. The stronger claim that creation becomes the most significant labour-market effect has not cleared those gates.

What the evidence shows

A shock needs an observable threshold

The scenario defines an enduring shock as an external disruption that materially constrains Australian demand, supply, public finances or access to critical technology for at least four consecutive quarters.

Supply networks can amplify disruption

Central-bank research supports examining indirect supply-chain effects as well as the initial shock, while not providing an Australia-specific forecast.

Software and robotics have different exposures

Software depends on offshore compute, currency and vendor decisions. Robotics adds hardware, components and concentrated critical-mineral supply chains.

Support must continue under stress

A resilient programme would need funding, delivery capacity, simple access, outcome measurement and a continuation rule tied to the shock trigger.

The cumulative claim still fails required gates

A plausible creation pathway remains, but the evidence does not establish dominance, sufficient support or observed survival through a qualifying shock.

What these verdicts do not mean

  • a major shock is certain
  • one of the four pathways is forecast as most likely
  • AI-enabled creation is impossible
  • support cannot improve resilience
  • the proposition is permanently false rather than open to retesting

Where this argument could be wrong

Official data may undercount portfolio work and informal transitions. A future shock could activate rather than weaken support, robotics costs could fall, or a well-designed programme could produce outcomes the current record cannot show. Conversely, creation may be less valuable than business counts suggest if income, survival or protections are weak.

Bottom line

The creation pathway is real enough to test and not strong enough to declare the largest effect. Targeted help may improve access, but dominance, sufficient support and shock survival are not established. The honest cumulative conclusion is NOT SUPPORTED ON CURRENT EVIDENCE, with clear conditions for retesting.
