Report 1 · Significance and impact thresholds

When Does It Count? Testing the Significance of Australia's AI Data-Centre Build-Out to 2035

  • Author: Rick Molony
  • Developed with AI assistance
  • Evidence to 24 June 2026
  • CC BY 4.0

Do the expected national and local impacts remain below the report's pre-committed significance thresholds?

Report 1 verdict

REJECTED AS A WHOLE

The build-out is nationally manageable on most axes, but fails the emissions test without additional renewables and fails locally where persistent breaches are evidenced. The report does not establish a breach at every named location.

What the evidence shows

National scale is material, not automatically system-breaking

The report's central case places data centres at about 8–11% of grid demand by 2035. That is large enough to require planning and coordination, but does not by itself establish national electricity-system failure.

Emissions become decisive without additional clean supply

The no-additionality case is approximately 5.86 Mt CO2-e a year, equivalent to about 2.5–3.2% of the 2035 target budget used in the report. This is a conditional scenario, not an observed outcome.

Local evidence carries the rejection

The report identifies persistent local pressure in the best-evidenced clusters, including Western Sydney and Melbourne's west, while treating the Northern Territory separately. It does not claim that every named location breaches every test.

Pipeline evidence is discounted

An Oxford Economics estimate that roughly six in seven requested megawatts may be phantom demand is informative, but it was commissioned by AWS and is not treated as a neutral forecast. Connection requests are not built load.

Additionality changes the result

The emissions conclusion depends on whether new demand is matched by genuinely new generation and storage. Annual certificate matching against existing supply is not enough for the report's public-interest test.

What this verdict does not mean

  • Every proposed project or every named location breaches the significance tests.
  • Every connection request becomes built and operating load.
  • Australia's national electricity or water systems necessarily fail.
  • Annual renewable certificates alone prove genuine additionality.
  • The qualitative local breach judgements are numeric measurements.

Where this argument could be wrong

The rejection could weaken if materially less of the pipeline is built, operators prove genuine additionality, or transparent local data overturns the assessed breaches. It could strengthen if observed load, emissions, water use or local infrastructure costs exceed the central case.

Bottom line

National manageability is not the same as public-interest acceptability. The whole hypothesis fails because the no-additionality emissions case and the best-evidenced local pressures cross the report's pre-committed tests.
